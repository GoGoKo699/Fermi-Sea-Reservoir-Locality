# Related work

Quadratic Hamiltonians with linear fermionic jump operators belong to the quasifree class of Markovian open systems. Their covariance dynamics, Gaussian stationary states and spectral solution are established tools; Barthel and Zhang provide a treatment that also resolves odd-parity sectors and nondiagonalizable generators [1]. In translation-invariant finite-range systems, the Bloch Lyapunov equation makes the stationary covariance a rational function of momentum variables. Zhang and Barthel use this structure to establish restrictions on critical stationary correlations [2]. We use this existing rational representation, with explicit degree accounting, in a finite-size global-accuracy problem.

Exact local stabilization has a thermodynamic obstruction. Zhang and Barthel discuss the impossibility of stabilizing a nontrivial pure Fermi sea with fixed finite-support Lindblad operators in Proposition 9.2 of their irreducibility study [3]. Tang, Barad and Wen derive a spectral criterion for exponentially local linear jumps in this setting, showing that sign-changing Fermi-level crossings obstruct exact ground-state preparation [4]. On a finite ring, jumps supported on the entire ring can stabilize the target exactly. The quantitative question addressed here is how much support is necessary at fixed nonzero global overlap when the generator may depend on system size.

Westhoff, Murciano and Moroder study the relation between jump localization and dissipative preparation time for quadratic fermionic systems [5]. Their construction suppresses the spatial tails of exact target-annihilating jumps at a dynamical cost, and they analyze finite-range truncations. Their Appendix F explicitly derives rational stationary populations and a product expression for the true overlap with the Fermi sea, including the amplification of truncation errors when both jump amplitudes become small. Our optimization instead ranges over all generators in the specified finite-range quasifree class and determines the minimum spatial range at fixed global overlap. The result does not establish a matching optimum for preparation time.

A closely related approximation problem occurs for Gaussian fermionic matrix product states. Franco-Rubio and Cirac prove that approximating the critical hopping-chain ground state to fixed global accuracy requires superpolynomial Gaussian-MPS bond dimension, although general fermionic MPS permit polynomial bond dimension [6]. They also derive rational momentum occupations and use logarithmically distributed polynomial roots in their numerical construction. Their discussion suggests a logarithm-squared virtual-mode scale, while their rigorous general lower bound establishes superpolynomial bond dimension. These results supply both a conceptual antecedent and an explicit rational-filter framework.

The approximation machinery is classical. Newman's construction achieves root-exponential rational approximation using geometrically distributed roots [7]. Sherstov proves sharp uniform rational sign-approximation bounds on a discrete arithmetic grid [8, Theorem 5.1]: in the relevant degree regime, the optimal error scales as $`\exp[-\Theta(d/\log(2n/d))]`$. Thus the occurrence of logarithm-squared degree at uniformly inverse-polynomial error is established approximation theory. The present converse must additionally accommodate the curved momentum grid and the aggregate occupation-error budget implied by global fidelity, which permits exceptional modes. The discrete root-deletion estimate addresses those features, and the upper construction realizes the resulting filters as positive gain and loss rates.

The resulting statement is a spatial resource theorem for one physical fermion mode per cell, translation-invariant time-independent quadratic Hamiltonians and linear jumps, and convergence to a unique physical stationary state. Pairing is included when coherent range is counted. Arbitrary finite coefficients and arbitrarily long waiting are permitted. For the explicit positive gain/loss construction, the [coefficient-rounding theorem](../science/reservoir_construction.md) proves that $`O(\log^3(N/\epsilon))`$ fractional bits per independent normalized coefficient suffice. At fixed requested infidelity $`0<\epsilon<1`$ and bounded occupation rates $`g(k)\le\Gamma`$, its [actual worst-case preparation time](../science/preparation_costs.md) is $`\Gamma^{-1}\exp[\Theta((\log N)^3)]`$, including the rounded realization. These time and precision statements characterize this construction; the spatial converse does not establish an optimized time or precision frontier over the full generator class.
